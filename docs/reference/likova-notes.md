# Likova reference notes

Sources: https://videinfra.com/work/likova (case study) and https://likova.space/ (live site, viewed with Playwright at 1440 and 390, frames in `docs/reference/likova/`). We copy the level of craft and the structure of the experience, never the text, images or code.

## What the case study says
- Concept: a Class A business center made of stepped glass and metal volumes. The stepped, offset planes become the recurring motif of the interface. Glass is the central metaphor.
- Three WebGL scenes, each with one job:
  1. An abstract glass object (faceted cube of offset volumes, refracted light).
  2. An explorable 3D building model: rotate it and details surface as you go.
  3. An interactive site plan you move through, not a static diagram.
- Office finder has two modes: a visual selector (pick a floor and layout) and a parametric filter (area, floor, layout).
- Scroll: stepped panels flow into each other and reshape. Parallax moves interior images at different speeds.
- Awwwards Site of the Day.

## What the live site shows (frames desktop-00..11, mobile-00..11)
- Opens on one huge wordmark and one photoreal night render of the building. Almost no copy on the first screen.
- Header is tiny: a two-line menu icon left, three small uppercase links right. Everything else lives in the off-canvas menu.
- A thin outlined frame with a big "A" and the word CLASS becomes a quiet graphic device. Small uppercase text, wide tracking.
- One idea per screen, lots of empty space, slow cinematic pacing, long scroll with numbered chapters.
- Interactions are modals and overlays (master plan, architect note, office selector), not more sections.

## Principles we translate to VDC Academy
1. One signature 3D object: the Stepped BIM Block (offset glass floors with BIM systems visible inside).
2. Few scenes, clear jobs: hero object, explorer (rotate, isolate discipline, clash, 4D, LOD), program finder.
3. One motif: stepped slabs for transitions, cards, form steps, learning path.
4. Restraint: 6 chapters instead of 13 sections, big type, few words.
5. Parallax depth on visuals.
6. Two ways to find a program: visual (floors) and parametric (filters).

## Differences we choose on purpose
- Our palette is Bay BIM Designs teal on dark, not Likova's light, white-and-navy look.
- Our building is procedural 3D (no photoreal renders available), so the glass and X-ray systems have to carry the premium feel.
- Education goal: the site must convert applicants, so Apply stays visible but calm.
